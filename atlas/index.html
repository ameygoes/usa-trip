    Charging plan, checked Oct 31

    • Dec 8, up to Yosemite. Charge to full at the El Portal Supercharger (11136 CA-140, 8 stalls, up to 250 kW) before you enter the Valley. The Valley has Level 2 chargers only, so bring the J1772 adapter. The Valley Lodge has 8, open to anyone, and you pay the park entrance fee to use them.
    • Dec 10, Yosemite to home. The Valley Lodge has Level 2 chargers, so the car fills overnight. Top up at El Portal on the way out if it is under about 80%. The 200-mile drive is inside the range I assumed.
    • Dec 20, Sunnyvale to LA. Expect one or two stops on the 380-mile drive, and Kettleman City is the classic one on I-5. Let the car’s navigation pick it.
    • Dec 21–23, LA and San Diego. Optional: the Tesla Diner Supercharger, 7001 Santa Monica Blvd, West Hollywood, 80 stalls. San Diego’s downtown Supercharger is in the 6th & A garage, but it is slow.
    • Dec 25, San Diego to home. Top up to about 90% at La Jolla Village Square (8657 Villa La Jolla Dr, 16 stalls) before you leave, and check the charger is up the day before, since Christmas Day is no time for an outage. Expect two stops on the 500-mile drive, and Kettleman City is the classic one on I-5.
    • Dec 29, Tahoe. South Lake Tahoe Supercharger (961 Emerald Bay Rd, 12 stalls, up to 325 kW). Placerville is the usual stop before the climb.
    • Dec 31, Monterey and Big Sur. The round trip is about 195 miles, inside the range I assumed, but cold and hills eat into it. Leave with a full battery and top up in Monterey or Carmel if the car asks. The Big Sur Supercharger at Ventana (48123 Highway 1, 8 stalls) tops out at 72 kW, so treat it as a backup.

    I assumed 250 to 300 miles of rated range, less in the cold and on the Sierra climbs. Outages happen, so check the Tesla app the day before each leg, and let the car’s navigation precondition the battery. Stall counts come from third-party trackers. Check your owner’s manual for chain-compatible tire sizes before carrying chains.
